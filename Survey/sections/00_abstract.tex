Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems increasingly rely on Large Language Models as automated evaluators due to the unscalable nature of human annotation. However, the reliability of these judges under the unique stressors of retrieval environments remains underexplored. This survey investigates the reasoning limitations of LLM evaluators by dissecting the conflict between truthfulness and faithfulness. We analyze how factors like extreme context lengths and retrieved noise trigger inherent model biases. Rather than merely documenting macro-level performance degradation, this paper explores the underlying mechanisms that disrupt knowledge reconciliation. Furthermore, we review the progression of mitigation strategies from static prompting interventions to multi-model debates. Ultimately, we argue for a necessary paradigm shift toward proactive, tool-augmented agentic frameworks capable of external verification. This analysis provides a grounded perspective for researchers seeking to design bias-resistant evaluation architectures.
