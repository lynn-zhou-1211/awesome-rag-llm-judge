Evaluating generative systems requires substantial human effort, driving the industry to adopt large language models as automated judges. While this shift enables scalable evaluation, it introduces new reliability concerns. Evaluators operating within retrieval augmented generation environments face specific contextual challenges such as distracting information, extreme document lengths, and positional biases. If these factors compromise the automated judge, the entire evaluation pipeline becomes untrustworthy.

Recent surveys approach automated evaluation from various perspectives. \citet{gu2025surveyllmasajudge} and \citet{li2025generationjudgmentopportunitieschallenges} outline common model vulnerabilities like positional and verbosity biases. Additionally, \citet{Brehme_2025} catalogs evaluation methodologies and datasets designed for retrieval augmented frameworks. However, these works primarily treat evaluation as a systems engineering challenge or a benchmarking task. They focus heavily on macro-level performance metrics rather than the underlying causes of evaluator failure.

This survey addresses these gaps by investigating the micro-level mechanisms of automated judges. First, we examine how environmental stressors like massive context windows and retrieved noise amplify evaluator bias. Next, the paper dissects the internal conflict between truthfulness and faithfulness to reveal how models fail when their pre-trained parametric knowledge contradicts retrieved evidence. Finally, we map the trajectory of evaluation methods, moving from static prompt engineering toward autonomous agentic frameworks that actively verify claims using external tools.
