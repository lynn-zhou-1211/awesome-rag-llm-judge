Evaluating generative systems requires substantial human effort, forcing the industry to adopt large language models as scalable automated judges. This shift dramatically improves efficiency, yet introduces critical reliability concerns. Specifically, when evaluators operate within retrieval augmented generation environments, they face unique challenges: contextual distractors, extreme document lengths, and positional biases. If the judge itself becomes compromised by these factors, the validity of the entire evaluation pipeline collapses.

Recent surveys have begun examining automated evaluation from different angles. Gu and colleagues \citet{gu2025surveyllmasajudge} and Li and coauthors \citet{li2025generationjudgmentopportunitieschallenges} document common evaluator vulnerabilities, including positional and verbosity biases. Brehme \citet{Brehme_2025} provides a comprehensive catalog of evaluation methodologies and datasets tailored to retrieval augmented frameworks. However, these overviews primarily treat evaluation as a systems engineering challenge or standard benchmarking task, focusing on macro level behaviors rather than underlying mechanisms.

We address three critical gaps by investigating the micro level pathology of automated judges. We examine how environmental stressors such as extreme context lengths and retrieved noise trigger and amplify evaluator bias. We dissect the internal conflict between truthfulness and faithfulness, revealing how judges struggle when parametric knowledge contradicts retrieved evidence. Finally, we trace the evolution from static prompt engineering to autonomous agentic frameworks that actively verify claims through external tool use.
