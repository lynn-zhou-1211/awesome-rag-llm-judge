\subsection{Defining the Paradox}

A core vulnerability of automated evaluators stems from the tension between their pre-trained parametric memory and externally retrieved evidence. Consequently, these models often struggle to distinguish general factuality from strict adherence to the given text. When an LLM judge assesses a generated output, it inherently draws upon its training corpus. When the provided context contradicts this internal baseline, the resulting conflict often yields inconsistent scoring.

Recent studies illustrate this dynamic clearly. For example, \citet{ming2025faithevallanguagemodelstay} show that even state-of-the-art models degrade severely when presented with counterfactuals that defy common knowledge. Although performance remains stable on niche or time-sensitive topics, directly contradicting core world knowledge causes a sharp drop in faithfulness. In such scenarios, GPT-4o's accuracy drops from 96.3\% to 47.5\%, even when the correct answer is fully derivable from the provided text.

Resolving this requires measuring truthfulness and faithfulness separately, rather than conflating them into a single score \citep{fadeeva2026faithfulnessawareuncertaintyquantificationfactchecking}. Evaluating truthfulness means measuring the model's internal entropy. This differs from earlier approaches like FActScore \citep{min2023factscorefinegrainedatomicevaluation} ,which check atomic claims against external sources. Faithfulness, by contrast, should be judged purely on logical entailment from the prompt. Adding nuance to this division, \citet{cattan2025draggedconflictsdetectingaddressing} propose a taxonomy of knowledge divergence, showing that different types of conflict require tailored solutions. Applying a one-size-fits-all approach actually harms evaluation quality; instead, specific conflicts must be categorized to accurately assess epistemic risk.

To pinpoint where these errors begin, \citet{liu2025truthfulragresolvingfactuallevelconflicts} map unstructured text into knowledge graph triplets. This isolates factual contradictions before they reach the evaluator, giving the model a clearer baseline for judgment.

\subsection{Internal Mechanisms of Conflict}

To understand why evaluators fail, we must look beyond the final output and examine the underlying neural mechanisms. Recent studies use representational probing to track activations at the exact moment a conflict occurs.

Research indicates that knowledge reconciliation occurs predominantly in the middle layers of the network. \citet{gao2025probinglatentknowledgeconflict} isolated a latent signal within these hidden states that predicts whether the model will favor its internal weights or the given text. Furthermore, \citet{zhao2025understanding} detail the competitive dynamics driving this process. This conflict inherently creates a trade-off between truthfulness and faithfulness, which leads to inconsistent scoring. By mapping attention flow and activation patterns, \citet{ye2026seeingconflicttransparentknowledge} make this process observable at the token level. Their findings confirm that evaluation failures follow predictable mechanistic pathways rather than arising randomly.

\subsection{Dynamic Reliance and Control}

These mechanistic insights enable targeted interventions. Researchers have since developed mitigation strategies ranging from architectural tweaks to inference-time adaptations.

At the architectural level, adjusting activation thresholds in specific layers provides fine-grained control over whether the model relies on parametric or contextual knowledge \citep{bi2025parametersvscontextfinegrained}. This approach helps suppress pre-training bias when evaluating specialized documents. Alternatively, \citet{lin2026resistingcontextualinterferencerag} take a training-based route, reinforcing core reasoning pathways to protect the model from noisy context.
Without the need for retraining, \citet{wang2025accommodateknowledgeconflictsretrievalaugmented} address real-world conflicts at inference time by dynamically adjusting confidence weights based on context quality. Taking this a step further, \citet{huo2025microactmitigatingknowledgeconflict} shift the focus to the model's own reasoning, prompting it to generate explicit intermediate steps to resolve discrepancies. This move toward active, self-directed resolution paves the way for the agentic frameworks discussed next.
