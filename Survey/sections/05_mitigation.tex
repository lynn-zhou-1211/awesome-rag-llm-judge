Although the use of large language models as evaluators is growing, their reliability remains vulnerable to bias, context length, and noise. Recent research proposes several mitigation strategies to improve the robustness and alignment of these judging systems. This section categorizes these interventions into five areas: rubric calibration, structured prompting, multi-agent evaluation, agentic verification, and hybrid approaches. These methods illustrate a shift from static prompt engineering toward dynamic systems capable of active external verification.

\subsection{Rubric and Criteria Calibration}
Ambiguous evaluation criteria frequently cause inconsistent scoring in automated judges. AutoCalibrate~\cite{autocalibrate} addresses this via a multi-stage, gradient-free approach that aligns the evaluator with human preference. Rather than explicitly modeling preferences or requiring extensive fine-tuning, the method iteratively refines scoring criteria through in-context learning. Prometheus 2~\cite{prometheus2} offers an open-source alternative that specializes in direct assessment and pairwise ranking using strictly custom criteria. This contrasts with proprietary models that often default to general attributes like helpfulness and harmlessness.

\subsection{Structured Prompting and Reasoning}
Structured prompts offer another layer of control by guiding the model through explicit reasoning steps. Rather than producing a single scalar score, the model evaluates responses across multiple dimensions like factual correctness and coherence. Prior work~\cite{comm_systems} shows specific prompting strategies can reduce the influence of superficial features like politeness or response length. These strategies include rubric deconstruction, rhetorical style normalization, bias-aware self-correction, and reference-anchored comparison.

\subsection{Multi-Agent and Peer-based Evaluation}
Single evaluators often inherit systematic biases from their training data. Multi-agent frameworks counter this by deploying several evaluators that interact and critique one another. Methods like Peer Rank and Peer Discussion~\cite{prd} enable models to iteratively rank and discuss candidate outputs, thereby reducing self-enhancement and position bias. Culturally aware frameworks such as MACD~\cite{macd} assign distinct cultural perspectives to different agents beyond standard Western viewpoints, utilizing consensus mechanisms to further minimize bias.

\subsection{Agentic and Stepwise Verification}
Recent work shifts toward agentic judging, treating evaluation as a multi-step process of planning, decomposition, and verification. Frameworks like Agent-as-a-Judge~\cite{agent_judge} analyze the entire execution trajectory rather than just the final answer. Auto-Eval Judge~\cite{auto_eval_judge} breaks complex tasks into sub-goals and validates each step independently through evidence-based reasoning. This modular design allows the judge to handle diverse tasks from coding to general question answering with higher accuracy than single-pass evaluators. Validating intermediate decisions stops error propagation and proves highly effective for complex tasks like code generation.

\subsection{Human-in-the-loop and Hybrid Evaluation}
Fully automated evaluation remains imperfect despite structural improvements. Hybrid pipelines combining automated judges with selective human oversight offer a necessary layer of reliability. In deployed systems, human reviewers handle edge cases flagged by low model confidence or high inter-agent disagreement. This routing scales the evaluation pipeline while maintaining accuracy on ambiguous queries. \citet{comm_systems} note that ensemble strategies can effectively filter noise before human review becomes necessary.
